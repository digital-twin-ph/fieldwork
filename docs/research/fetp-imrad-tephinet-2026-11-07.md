# IMRaD as the FETP writing course teaches it

## What each part is for, and how the course says to build it

| Part | Its job | How to build it | Common faults the course names |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Introduction** | Show the gap in knowledge, why filling it matters, and what this study adds | Two moves. The **problem**: background to the gap, why addressing it is useful, and the limits of earlier studies. The **purpose**: background to the approach, what is new in it, and which earlier limits it overcomes. Funnel from broad to narrow. End on the objective, in the last sentence. About three paragraphs | A history of the issue; the whole literature review; too much general background; results or discussion; citing every paper when a few will do |
| **Methods** | Say exactly how the study was done, so a reader could repeat it and judge its credibility | Clear subheadings (often the journal's). Describe what you did, not what you found. Define every analysis variable and how it was measured. Detail original methods; cite standard ones. Chronological where it helps. A flow diagram for how the sample was reached | Results creeping in (a brief result is acceptable only where it explains a method choice, stated reason first); undefined or inconsistent variables; methods with no matching result; methods that disagree with the abstract |
| **Results** | State the findings that bear on the objective, without interpretation | Mirror the methods: every measurement described there has a finding here, in the same order or under matching subheadings, or from whole population to subgroups, or from simple to complex analysis. Draft tables and figures first, then text around them: a sentence or two per table or figure, with the right statistics. Past tense | Interpreting; restating whole tables in text; text that can be read two ways against its table; table arithmetic that does not add up; the same data in a table and a figure |
| **Discussion** | Interpret the results and justify the interpretation, in the light of the literature and the gap the study set out to fill | Aim for four paragraphs: (1) the key result and the main conclusion, each conclusion beside the result that supports it; (2) the study against comparable studies, and what is new and useful; (3) the major limitations, plainly and without apology, with what was done about them; (4) recommendations for practice or policy, and feasible next studies. Present tense | Findings not in the Results; new results; conclusions or recommendations the study does not support; losing the thread back to the gap |

## What this suggests for Fieldwork, not yet decided

- **The course's structure maps onto the notes model.** Its Introduction moves are the `context` and `rationale`
  functions; Methods is `source` and `method`; its Discussion paragraphs 3 and 4 are `limitation` and a function the
  notes do not yet name, recommendation.
- **Its central rule is the same seam [183](../experiments/183-forms-of-a-note.md) is about**: Results report without
  interpreting, Discussion interprets and must stay inside what the results support. Its fault lists are, almost
  item for item, candidates for the guide questions and for the plain checks.
- **It is a teaching course, not evidence that it works.** No outcome evaluation of it was found
  ([teaching writing from sources](teaching-writing-from-sources-2026-11-07.md)).

## What this does not establish

That FETP programmes everywhere teach IMRaD this way: this is one course, though the one CDC and TEPHINET offer to all
FETP residents. What modules 1–3 and 8 say, including the abstract. Whether the course's guidance improves trainees'
papers.
