## Purpose

A design-to-computation reference for implementing epidemiological analysis in FieldWork. Study designs determine sampling, estimands, valid computations, uncertainty methods, and interpretability. Spatial analysis and simulation are often *analytical methods*, not substitutes for an epidemiological study design.

## Study design taxonomy

| Family | Design | Primary question | Typical computations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Case report / case series | What happened among observed cases? | Counts, proportions, medians, onset intervals, epidemic curves |
| Descriptive | Cross-sectional survey | How common is a condition now? | Prevalence, prevalence ratio/difference, weighted CIs |
| Descriptive | Repeated cross-sectional surveys | How does prevalence change? | Standardized trends, regression, age-period patterns |
| Descriptive | Surveillance | What is occurring over place and time? | Incidence rates, moving averages, aberration detection, completeness |
| Observational | Ecological study | Are group-level exposures associated with outcomes? | Correlation, area-level rate ratios, spatial regression |
| Observational | Case-control | What prior exposures differ between cases and controls? | Odds ratio, conditional/unconditional logistic regression |
| Observational | Nested case-control | What exposures predict outcomes within a cohort? | Matched odds ratio; rate-ratio interpretation under risk-set sampling |
| Observational | Case-cohort | How do exposures relate to multiple cohort outcomes? | Weighted hazard/rate models |
| Observational | Prospective/retrospective cohort | What happens after exposure? | Risk ratio, risk difference, incidence rate ratio, survival, hazard ratio |
| Observational | Case-crossover | Does transient exposure trigger an acute event? | Matched odds ratio, conditional logistic regression |
| Observational | Self-controlled case series | Does event incidence change during exposure periods? | Within-person incidence rate ratio |
| Experimental | Individually randomized trial | Does assigned intervention work? | Intention-to-treat effect, risk difference/ratio, survival analysis |
| Experimental | Cluster randomized trial | Does a group-level intervention work? | Cluster-adjusted effects, ICC, design effect |
| Experimental | Stepped-wedge cluster trial | Does randomized phased rollout work? | Time- and cluster-adjusted intervention effects |
| Quasi-experimental | Interrupted time series | Did level or trend change after an intervention? | Segmented regression, level/slope change, autocorrelation |
| Quasi-experimental | Difference-in-differences | Did outcomes change differently in exposed groups? | Difference in changes, event study, pre-trend diagnostics |
| Quasi-experimental | Regression discontinuity | What is the effect near an eligibility threshold? | Local treatment effect, bandwidth sensitivity |
| Quasi-experimental | Instrumental variables | Can an instrument identify a causal exposure effect? | Two-stage estimators, IV effect, sensitivity tests |
| Diagnostic | Diagnostic accuracy | How accurately does a test classify disease? | Sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, likelihood ratios, ROC/AUC |
| Evidence synthesis | Systematic review/meta-analysis | What do multiple studies collectively show? | Pooled effects, heterogeneity, prediction intervals |
| Modeling | Compartmental / metapopulation | How might transmission evolve? | ODE/stochastic simulation, attack rate, R0, Rt, scenarios |
| Modeling | Agent-based | How do heterogeneous contacts affect transmission? | Monte Carlo epidemic distributions, intervention contrasts |

## Core epidemiological calculations

For a 2×2 table with exposed cases `a`, exposed noncases `b`, unexposed cases `c`, and unexposed noncases `d`:

- Exposed risk = `a/(a+b)`; unexposed risk = `c/(c+d)`.
- Risk ratio (RR) = `[a/(a+b)] / [c/(c+d)]`.
- Risk difference (RD) = `a/(a+b) - c/(c+d)`.
- Odds ratio (OR) = `(a*d)/(b*c)` for nonzero relevant cells.
- Incidence rate = `incident cases / person-time at risk`.
- Incidence rate ratio (IRR) = `rate_exposed / rate_unexposed`.
- Point prevalence = `existing cases / population at specified time`.
- Cumulative incidence = `new cases / initially at-risk population` over defined follow-up (with suitable handling of censoring and competing risks).
- Case-fatality proportion = `deaths among identified cases / identified cases`, with explicit follow-up window.
- Attributable fraction among exposed = `(RR-1)/RR` **only with defensible causal interpretation**.
- Direct age-standardized rate = `Σ(reference_weight_i × stratum_rate_i)`.
- Standardized mortality ratio = `observed deaths / expected deaths`.

**Critical:** Case-control sampling generally does not identify population risk from sample proportions. An OR is not automatically an RR. Every rate needs a denominator, unit, and observation period.

## Computations by outcome and data structure

| Data type | Typical estimators/models | Essential diagnostics |
|---|---|---|
| Binary outcome | Logistic, log-binomial, modified Poisson with robust SE | Separation, model fit, clustering |
| Count/rate | Poisson with log-exposure offset; negative binomial | Overdispersion, offset validity |
| Continuous | Linear regression, mixed models | Residuals, heteroscedasticity, functional form |
| Time-to-event | Kaplan–Meier, Cox, competing-risk methods | Censoring, proportional hazards where relevant |
| Repeated measures | GEE, mixed-effects models | Within-unit dependence |
| Complex survey | Weighted prevalence/regression | Strata, PSU, weights, nonresponse |
| Spatially dependent | Spatial regression, CAR/BYM, scan statistics | Spatial dependence, MAUP, multiple testing |
| Environmental time series | Poisson/GAM, distributed-lag nonlinear model | Seasonality, autocorrelation, confounding |
| Multi-study evidence | Fixed/common-effect or random-effects meta-analysis | Heterogeneity, study bias, dependent estimates |

## Design-specific implementation notes

### Descriptive and cross-sectional

Compute frequency distributions, standardized rates, weighted prevalence, confidence intervals, and subgroup contrasts. Check case definitions, nonresponse, survey design, and denominator quality. Cross-sectional association does not establish exposure–outcome temporal order.

### Case-control

Compute OR with uncertainty; use conditional logistic regression for matched sets. Preserve how controls were sampled. With incidence-density sampling, an appropriately estimated OR can estimate an incidence rate ratio without requiring a rare disease assumption.

### Cohort

Compute cumulative incidence, person-time rates, RR, RD, IRR, survival curves, HR, and adjusted estimates. Check immortal-time bias, time-varying exposure, informative censoring, loss to follow-up, and competing events.

### Randomized trials

Compute prespecified intention-to-treat contrasts and confidence intervals. For clusters, account for intracluster correlation and the number of randomized units. Stepped-wedge analyses must address calendar-time effects.

### Quasi-experimental

- **Interrupted time series:** fit baseline level and trend, intervention level change, post-intervention slope change; model seasonality and serial correlation.
- **Difference-in-differences:** `(treated_post − treated_pre) − (control_post − control_pre)`; investigate parallel trends, spillovers, and staggered-adoption estimators.
- **Regression discontinuity:** estimate effects locally at the cutoff; check manipulation, bandwidth, and continuity assumptions.
- **Instrumental variables:** check relevance, exclusion restriction, independence, and interpretation of the identified population effect.

### Diagnostic studies

Given `TP`, `FP`, `FN`, `TN`:

- Sensitivity = `TP/(TP+FN)`; specificity = `TN/(TN+FP)`.
- PPV = `TP/(TP+FP)`; NPV = `TN/(TN+FN)`.
- LR+ = `sensitivity/(1-specificity)`; LR− = `(1-sensitivity)/specificity`.

Assess verification bias, spectrum effects, reference-standard validity, calibration where relevant, and prevalence dependence of predictive values.

### Spatial epidemiology

Compute mapped rates with appropriate denominators, standardization, smoothing, global/local Moran's I, spatial scan statistics, accessibility, and spatial regression. Record boundary vintages, uncertainty, ecological fallacy, and modifiable areal unit problem (MAUP).

### Climate epidemiology

Compute temperature/rainfall anomalies relative to a specified baseline, lagged exposure features, exposure–response curves, distributed-lag cumulative effects, and adjusted rate ratios. Control for seasonality, secular trend, reporting changes, and spatial/temporal autocorrelation. Do not interpret predictive associations as causal without additional assumptions.

### Transmission and agent-based models

Compute epidemic curves, attack rates, peaks, scenario contrasts, parameter sensitivity, and stochastic uncertainty across seeds. Under homogeneous SIR assumptions, `R0 = beta/gamma`; structured models require a next-generation operator. Estimating `Rt` from cases requires an assumed generation-interval distribution and attention to ascertainment. **A cholera water-company mortality ratio is not R0.** Agent-based outputs require synthetic population and contact-network validation.

### Meta-analysis

Compute study-level effect estimates and SEs, inverse-variance pooling, between-study variance `tau²`, heterogeneity `I²`, and prediction intervals. Examine risk of bias and incompatible estimands before pooling.

## Minimum validation and acceptance criteria

1. Validate schema, geographic identifiers, dates, and units before computation.
2. Refuse to calculate risk/rate without a valid denominator or person-time.
3. Select effect measures appropriate to sampling and study design.
4. Distinguish observed, modeled, synthetic, and imputed data.
5. Supply confidence/credible intervals or explain why unavailable.
6. Preserve reproducibility: dataset versions, model specifications, software versions, and seeds.
7. Include missingness, bias, confounding, and sensitivity diagnostics.
8. Label simulation predictions and causal claims according to evidence and assumptions.
9. Test numerical formulas against known reference examples.
10. Expose study-design-specific warnings in the FieldWork UI.

### Evidence and implementation cautions

1. Reporting guidelines (STROBE, CONSORT, PRISMA, STARD, TRIPOD) **do not validate the design or estimator**.
2. R0, Rt, risk ratios, odds ratios, hazard ratios, and incidence rate ratios are distinct estimands; never substitute them automatically.
3. Spatial statistics require specified weights/neighborhoods, geographic support, and multiplicity decisions.
4. Climate lag models require seasonality, long-term trend, and confounder adjustment plus explicit lag-window choices.
5. ABM outputs depend on synthetic-population assumptions, contact structure, seeds, and parameter calibration.
6. This reference list is curated, not an exhaustive systematic search. Verify publication metadata and current guideline versions before a formal protocol or manuscript.
