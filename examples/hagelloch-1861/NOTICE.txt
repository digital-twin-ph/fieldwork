Bundled data for the Hagelloch 1861 measles example
===================================================

188 individual measles cases from Hagelloch, Germany, in the winter of 1861-62.

SOURCE
  Collected by Albert Pfeilsticker, MD thesis, Tubingen, 1863.
  Re-analysed and augmented by Heike Oesterle, MD thesis, Tubingen, 1992.
  The 1863 original: https://archive.org/details/beitrgezurpatho00pfeigoog
  Reached this project through the outbreaks R package 1.9.0, which took it from
  the surveillance package, which had it from Niels Becker via Peter J. Neal.

LICENCE
  No holder in that chain states one. Checked 2026-10-17 across surveillance,
  outbreaks and EpiCompare: each cites the two theses and gives no terms of reuse.

  This project treats the data as public domain. The 1863 source is out of
  copyright by age; the records are facts about an outbreak; the table has passed
  openly between studies for thirty years. That is a judgement about what is
  copyrightable, not a licence anybody granted, and the reasoning is recorded in
  docs/research/data-sources.md so a reader can disagree with it.

  Cite the chain if you use this: Pfeilsticker 1863, Oesterle 1992, and the
  package the bytes came from.

WHAT THIS PROJECT ADDED
  A georeference. x_loc and y_loc are metres from an arbitrary local origin, so
  nothing could be mapped without anchoring them. They are anchored at Hagelloch,
  a district of Tubingen, 48.54083 N 9.06056 E.

  THAT ANCHOR IS OURS, NOT THE SOURCE'S. The relative positions of the houses are
  Pfeilsticker's; where the village sits on the earth is this project's addition,
  accurate to the village and not to the building. x_loc and y_loc travel
  unchanged on every record, so the original frame is recoverable.
